## 14 October: GPU (RTX 5050, 8 GB)

Verified that real work runs on the card, not just that CUDA is installed: PyTorch matrix multiply 6.9 TFLOPS fp32 (12 with TF32) with results matching the CPU; a Warp kernel 18× faster than on the CPU with identical results; the cloth simulator reports the RTX 5050 as its device and VRAM rises while it runs.

| | Before (CPU) | Now (GPU) |
|---|---|---|
| T-shirt drape, same settings | 184 s | 8.3 s (22×) |
| Trousers drape, self-collision on | 284 s (so it was switched off: 61 s) | 9.3 s (31×) |
| Default cloth | 1.6 cm; trousers without self-collision | 1 cm, self-collision on: tee 17.8 s, trousers 23.7 s, under 0.5 GB VRAM |
| Full kit from Thread Studio (body + photo tee + trousers + overshirt) | 15+ min at the lower quality | about 5.5 min at 1 cm |

Seen in Thread Studio: 1 cm cloth removes the ragged neckline and sleeve hems that 1.6 cm showed in a relaxed pose; trousers and the tee now carry a woven/knit surface (fabric normal maps) instead of smooth plastic; the inside-of-cloth shading no longer leaves dark slivers along side seams. Studio shows the GPU, its memory and temperature live, each job's device and duration, and a cloth-quality choice (automatic picks 1 cm on a GPU).

Kept on the CPU, because measurement says so: the body build (fits are sequential steps on one small mesh; 90 s on the CPU vs 231 s on the GPU). Its 52 expressions are now computed in one batch (14 s → 3 s). Garment photo cut-out runs U2-Net on the CPU (about 6 s per photo); GPU inference would need the CUDA build of ONNX Runtime.

Not yet on the GPU: photo reconstruction. The installed COLMAP is the official no-CUDA build; the engine and setup now use the CUDA build when it is installed (415 MB download, awaiting your approval). Phone cost of 1 cm garments: a full outfit is about 75–100k triangles and 1–1.4 MB per garment; not yet measured on an iPhone, and the fabric normal maps use mipmaps on the phone for the first time.

## How to look at it on Windows

Double-click **`Thread-Studio.cmd`**. It starts the local engine and opens Thread Studio in your browser: the 3D fitting room plus the asset-factory controls. Pick a kit, dress it, orbit or use the camera presets (front, ¾, side, back, face, torso, feet), switch poses (relaxed breathing, walk, arms out, knee bend), faces (52 expressions), lighting, wireframe and skeleton, and save screenshots. Enter measurements to build your body and add garments with flat-lay photos; jobs run on this PC and finished pieces appear automatically.

## Still not verified

- **Real photos.** The selfie test uses a synthetic render (grey brows, closed eyes) and the garment test uses a synthetic striped tee. Real selfies and real flat-lays have never been run; texture projection will need tuning on them.
- **On an iPhone.** Rendering of the kit on expo-gl (skinning, 52 morph targets, now about 50k triangles and a 2048 px skin texture), frame rate, memory and touch. The bundled kit grew by about 1.2 MB (base layer, larger skin texture).
- **TrueDepth scanning.** The Swift module compiles in CI; it has never run on a device.
- **Hairline.** Hair is a CC0 card mesh; from close up the front edge still looks cut out. The scalp under the hair now takes the hair colour, which hides gaps but not the edge.
- **Long sleeves and outerwear over tops** have not been inspected as closely as tees and trousers.
